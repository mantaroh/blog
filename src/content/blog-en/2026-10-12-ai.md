# My Child Said, “I Want to Use Chappy,” So I Made an AI Chat for the Family

My child told me, “I want to use Chappy.”

I use AI for work on a regular basis, and I also research AI and web technologies in graduate school.

So I have not prohibited my children from using AI either. But when it came time for my own children to use it, I found myself thinking about all sorts of things.

**I want them to be able to use AI. But I don’t want them to rely on AI for everything.**

So I decided that our family would come up with some rules, and that I would build a chat service where we could put those rules into practice.

That is how I created a service called [Ouchi AI](https://home-ai.mantaroh.com/).

## Is It Okay to Send a Friend’s Photo to AI?

For example, there was a story I heard about school involving someone uploading a friend’s photo to ChatGPT and editing it. The story itself was from a little while ago, and these days the service itself prevents users from carrying out that operation when they upload personal photos.

If you show a photo to this AI, it can describe or edit the photo. I think these are fun features for children.

But the photo may show a friend’s face.

It is not simply a matter of being free to use a photo because it is your own. You also have to consider the privacy of the people appearing in it.

The other point is homework.

If you take a photo of a problem and give it to AI, it will tell you the answer.

Of course, there is nothing wrong with having an AI explain a problem you do not understand. In fact, I would like children to use it for that purpose.

However, I also find myself wondering what children will learn if they leave only the task of producing answers to AI.

**What can we give to AI? What can we leave to AI?**

I feel these are important questions for children going forward.

## Simply Choosing Not to Let Them Use It Does Not Feel Quite Right Either

At first, I did consider simply not letting my children use AI because it is dangerous.

But is that really enough?

Children have privacy too. They may have worries they do not want their parents to know about, or things they want to think through on their own.

There is the desire to protect our children, and there is also the desire to respect their privacy. Balancing the two is difficult.

And I think parents can only manage everything while their children are still young.

I also have a policy of not wanting to raise my children by managing everything about them in the first place.

As they grow, they will eventually start choosing services for themselves. At that point, what they will need is not restrictions set by their parents, but **the ability to judge risks for themselves and to value their own information and that of others**.

## We Set Rules as a Family and Built a Chat Service

So first, we decided to discuss how to use AI as a family.

What should we do when sending someone’s photo? How should we think about using AI for homework? What kinds of information will be sent to the internet?

Rather than unilaterally imposing answers as parents, I wanted us to think about these things together.

And the place I created for putting those rules into practice is “Ouchi AI.”

### Thinking for Yourself About Where to Send Information

With Ouchi AI, users can choose between different AI execution environments: Cloudflare and OpenAI.

Both are AIs on the internet, but the destinations to which information is sent are different.

What is important is not simply being able to switch between AIs.

It is being conscious of where the text and photos you enter will be sent.

We also let users choose the AI’s capabilities.

I wanted to accommodate requests to use a high-performance AI, like the paid version of Chappy. However, the points required and the things each AI is good at differ depending on which AI you use.

A high-performance AI is not necessarily the right choice for everything.

### Learning Information Literacy Once a Month

We have also created a system for learning about topics including online game chats, social media, personal information, scams, and impersonation—not just AI.

Once a month, we provide an opportunity to learn about these kinds of topics.

To be honest, adults do not understand everything either. New services and new problems continue to emerge.

For example, late last year, the Children and Families Agency and the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology issued several warnings concerning AI.

- Children and Families Agency: [Practical Handbook for Introducing and Utilizing Generative AI](https://www.cfa.go.jp/assets/contents/node/basic_page/field_ref_resources/c1890510-04d4-497b-9e23-a7f514016c7d/04f2c133/20250709_councils_kodomo_seisaku_DX_40.pdf)
- Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology: [Guidelines for the Effective Use of Generative AI in Primary and Secondary Education (Ver. 2.0)](https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/other/mext_02412.html)

Both contain very useful information, but in the AI industry, six months ago is ancient history. New points of concern have emerged that are on an entirely different level from the things that needed attention at the time. Specifically,

- Echo chambers caused by AI responses
- Advertising by free AI services
- Investigations resulting from writing threats in AI chats

are among the many problems that have continued to arise even as of October 2026.

That is why I believe parents need to keep learning together with their children as new issues arise.

### Not Constantly Monitoring Every Conversation

Ouchi AI is not premised on parents constantly reading and managing their children’s chat content.

Of course, safety measures necessary for children’s use need to be considered separately.

Packet filtering and blocking and monitoring functions have long been available as features of mobile phones for children and similar services.

I believe that children who have reached a certain age need privacy, and that parents should not know every conversation they have.

I want to maintain a relationship in which my children can come to me for advice when something troubling happens.

For that reason as well, I believe it is important for parents and children to talk regularly.

## Something Different from Internet Education in the Past Will Be Needed

When I first started using the internet, I interacted with people on the other side of the screen through bulletin boards, chats, email, and so on. In particular, during the heyday of 2ch and Nico Nico Douga, I was aware that I needed to be careful about handling personal information.

After social media became widespread, other issues emerged, such as information posted online spreading beyond your control and the difficulty of taking back information once it had been made public.

And now, AI.

AI talks like a human, listens to our concerns, and even creates text and images for us.

Unlike the internet of the past, the entity responding on the other side of the screen is not a human, but an intelligent AI modeled after humans.

That is precisely why, in addition to education about protecting information, we will also need education about **how to interpret AI’s answers and how much to entrust to it**.

The Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology has also published guidelines on the effective use of generative AI. However, the existence of guidelines for schools and how each individual family should engage with AI are separate issues.

Literacy is gradually changing now, but I think the reality is that we are still in the middle of a process where systems have not yet been fully established around the world.

## Parents Also Need to Stay Informed

Another thing I created is a publication called [Ouchi AI News](https://home-ai.mantaroh.com/media/).

We research what is happening on the internet in areas close to children’s daily lives, including AI, social media, and online games, and summarize the points that parents and children should be aware of.

The five most recent articles can be read in full without registering as a member. Earlier articles are available to members.

In these articles, I want to focus not only on introducing dangers, but also on the perspectives of “how should we think about this at home?” and “what should we talk about with our children?”

I am also still learning as I research these topics.

## Not for Profit

Ouchi AI originally began as something I was making for my own family.

As a software engineer involved in development and a graduate student researching computer science, I thought that if I could build it myself, I might as well give it a try.

Of course, using AI incurs costs such as API fees. For that reason, we have adopted a points system based on usage.

However, pursuing profit as a business was not the starting point for this development.

The things I have struggled with while raising my children are probably things that other families will have opportunities to think about as well.

With that in mind, I decided to make public what I had started for my own family.

Rather than being a completed educational service, it is an initiative that we will continue improving as our family uses it and thinks about it together.

## I Want to Value Conversations Between Parents and Children

AI will become an even more familiar presence in the future, and I do not believe that talking with AI itself is by any means a bad thing.

However, if children start consulting AI about everything and end up having fewer opportunities to talk with the people close to them, that would be a little sad.

Talking with other people. Thinking about their feelings. Expressing your own thoughts.

And thinking and making decisions for yourself.

I do not think these things will become unnecessary simply because AI has become more convenient.

What we aim for with Ouchi AI is not just to increase conversations between children and AI.

**It is for conversations between parents and children to increase as a result of conversations with AI.**

I believe that is the most important thing.

We are still in the process of trial and error, but I would be happy if people facing similar questions could use it too.
