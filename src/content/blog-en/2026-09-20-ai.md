In the doctoral program I am enrolled in, I am researching the automatic generation of Web content using AI.

I began thinking about my research plan in late 2022.

However, this field evolved far too quickly.

Every time I moved my research forward, something would happen that made me think, “Hasn’t what I was trying to research become something that can just be done normally now?” Each time, I adjusted my research topic and methods.

It is quite challenging as doctoral research, but when I think of it as observing the evolution of generative AI from 2022 to 2026 while being swept along with my research topic, it has been an interesting experience in its own way.

I want to leave a record of that experience.

## The ChatGPT Shock During the Admissions Process

Around October 2022, I was preparing to apply to a doctoral program.

Because I did not have a master’s degree, I also had to undergo an admissions eligibility screening to enter the doctoral program directly from a bachelor’s degree.

At the time, my research plan involved generating Web pages using Computer Vision.

The idea was to crawl websites, extract and classify HTML components, and reconstruct Web pages based on the results.

However, right in the middle of the admissions process, ChatGPT was released on November 30, 2022.

I knew about GPT-3, but ChatGPT was quite a shock.

Simply by giving instructions in natural language, it could generate not only text but also code.

Even before enrolling, I started thinking, “Maybe I should change my research plan?”

However, at the time, the focus of LLMs was still natural language.

I thought there was still room to approach the visual understanding and design of Web pages through Computer Vision, so I proceeded to the doctoral program with my original plan.

## The Shock of Multimodal LLMs

As a working professional pursuing a doctorate, I decided to use the long-term study system and take five years to earn my PhD.

Looking back, making a five-year plan centered on generative AI as a research subject was quite a bold choice.

From the latter half of 2023 through 2024, multimodal LLMs capable of handling images rapidly became practical.

Until then, I had thought of the boundary as:

“Images are for Computer Vision”  
“Text is for LLMs”

That boundary became considerably more blurred.

If you give a screenshot to an LLM and ask, “What is the structure of this UI?” it will return a reasonably good answer.

As a result, my research also shifted from being centered on CV to using LLMs for Web page understanding and generation.

That said, at the time, LLMs were still far from being able to create a finished website simply by asking them to “make a nice-looking website.”

They could create simple HTML, but complex layouts and interactions tended to break down.

So I adopted an approach that combined semantic understanding by an LLM with a Web component classifier built from crawled data.

Rather than leaving everything to AI, the system had the AI choose from a set of relatively predefined Web components.

## The Shock of Figma Sites

After that, I completed a classifier using Common Crawl data and proceeded as far as experiments in Web page generation.

The next problem was design.

You can specify “what to make” in natural language, but it is difficult to express “how to present it” using only natural language.

So I used the Figma API to build a system based on:

**Figma → Analysis of design structure → Web site generation**

And, as you know, Figma Sites was announced in May 2025.

My reaction when I saw the announcement was, “Again?”

Something quite close to the direction of my research had appeared as a normal product.

Figma Make appeared as well.

Whenever I settled on a research topic, a product would appear.

I gradually started to wonder whether I might have some kind of ability to make that happen.

Of course, I do not have any such ability.

Once again, I had to adjust the direction of my research.

## From “One-Shot Generation” to “Iterative Generation”

However, problems still remained.

It is difficult to generate a Web site correctly in one shot.

This is the same in human development.

Build it.

Look at it.

Fix it.

Run it again.

So rather than competing based solely on the result of a single generation, I focused on a REPL-like approach that repeatedly performs:

**Generation → Execution → Evaluation → Correction**

The center of my research also shifted from **whether it is possible to generate Web pages** to **whether AI itself can evaluate and improve the Web pages it generates**.

## The Shock of Multi-Agent Systems

And then came 2026.

This time, it became considerably more realistic to build systems by combining multiple AI agents.

Even for Web site generation alone, tasks such as the following can be assigned to separate agents:

- Think through the requirements
- Design
- Write the code
- Run it in a browser
- Evaluate the screen
- Check accessibility

A few years ago, I was thinking about “how to choose the right components,” but now I am thinking about “who should be assigned to do what.”

It is starting to feel less like a research lab and more like managing an AI development team.

## Has the Research Topic Disappeared?

As the technology progresses this far, I sometimes wonder, “Is there anything left to research?” At the same time, I have also sometimes felt that we have entered a world where it is only natural for research conceived by an ordinary person like me to become commonplace in commercial products.

But in reality, the opposite happened.

When I began my research, the question was **“Can AI generate Web sites?”**

As of 2026, this question itself is gradually being answered.

That leads to the next questions.

Are the generated Web sites correct?

Do they meet the requirements?

Are they easy to use?

How should they be evaluated, and how should they be improved?

Rather than the research topic disappearing, it feels as though the question has advanced one step further.

## So, What Am I Researching Now?

I have adjusted my topic many times along the way.

Ideally, I would like to try everything whenever a new model comes out.

I want to work on multi-agent systems as well.

I want to explore new generation methods.

There will probably be something else interesting by the time I graduate.

However, doctoral programs have graduation deadlines.

If I keep chasing technological progress, I will never finish my doctoral dissertation.

So now I have narrowed down my research subject and am working on **evaluating and improving Japanese-specific Web accessibility for Web pages generated by LLMs**.

LLMs have become capable of generating very polished Web pages.

But **being able to create something and being able to use it are different things.**

The HTML is correct.

It looks good.

It works in a browser.

Even so, accessibility issues remain.

There are also aspects that depend on the Japanese language and context, such as how natural the content sounds when read aloud, the meaning of headings and links, and how easy the text is to understand.

Can these aspects be evaluated and improved using LLMs?

That is what I am researching now.

Looking back, in 2022 I was thinking about **“whether AI could create Web sites.”**

Now I am thinking about **“how to ensure the quality of what AI creates, assuming that it will create it.”**

The question has changed quite a bit.

My research topic has been shaken up many times by the evolution of generative AI, but I am enjoying that situation quite a lot.

Whenever a new technology appears, I think:

“Once again, my research topic is in danger.”

And I usually try it first.

However, I really need to write my doctoral dissertation soon.

So for now, while keeping an eye on new technologies, I want to properly delve into the single topic of Japanese Web accessibility.

Until I graduate, I hope major technological innovations can wait just a little longer, if possible.
